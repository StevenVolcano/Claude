The first thirty minutes of the day have an outsized influence on emotional tone. The nervous system wakes up in a state of mild alertness — cortisol rises naturally in the morning as a mobilizing signal. What happens in that early window either works with that biology or works against it.

Checking your phone immediately upon waking is one of the most effective ways to prime the threat-detection system before the day has properly begun. Social media, news, and messages are each designed to capture attention through urgency, novelty, and emotional activation. Encountering them as the first input of the day means the stress-response circuitry — the sympathetic nervous system — gets activated before the prefrontal cortex has fully come online. The emotional tone this sets can persist for hours.

The alternative is not an elaborate two-hour morning routine. Complexity is the enemy of consistency. A practice that requires an hour of uninterrupted time will be abandoned the moment life becomes busy — which is exactly when you most need it. The case for a simple, brief morning practice is a case for sustainability over impressiveness.

Three components form a useful minimum. A short breath practice — one to two minutes of conscious, slower-than-usual breathing — begins to shift the nervous system from the sympathetic (mobilized) state toward the parasympathetic (settled) state. A single intention — one sentence that names how you want to show up today, not what you want to accomplish — orients the day toward values rather than tasks. One gratitude — specific, not generic — activates the parts of the brain associated with positive affect and counteracts the negativity bias that threat-detection creates.

The entire practice can take three minutes. That is the point.

## Exercise: The 3-Minute Morning Set

Before touching your phone tomorrow morning, sit with your feet on the floor.

**Minute 1 — Breath:** Breathe in for four counts, hold for one, breathe out for six. Repeat four times. Notice your body, not your to-do list.

**Minute 2 — Intention:** Complete this sentence in your mind or on paper: "Today I want to show up as someone who..." Keep it behavioral and specific.

**Minute 3 — Gratitude:** Name one specific thing you are genuinely grateful for. Not a category ("my family"), but a particular instance ("the way my partner laughed last night").

That is it. Do this for seven days before evaluating whether it is worth continuing.
